This results in undesirable environmental, material, and social consequences, such as the waste of natural resources, accumulation of waste, and the failure to benefit from food despite the existence of segments suffering from poverty. In recent years, several initiatives and electronic platforms have emerged to reduce food waste, but most of these treatments have focused on restaurants only, without involving other pillars of society such as families and non-profit organizations. The lack of coordination and unified supervision has also led to inconsistencies in procedures for donating or distributing food unfairly. This effort aims to introduce a comprehensive digital treatment that expands the concept of reducing food waste to include restaurants, homes, charities, and individuals, through an organized and user-friendly program. The system enables the display of excess food, arrangement of receipt and distribution procedures, while providing supervisory roles for follow-up, authentication, and resolving conflicts between inquiries. The project also focuses on the humanitarian aspect, as it provides the option of obtaining food for free or at a low price, which contributes to preserving the dignity of the needy and strengthening community solidarity. By relying on modern technologies, this work aims to support environmental stability and achieve optimal benefit from food resources.Aim and ObjectivesThe objective of this project is to develop a practical application of a system to help combat the issue of food wastage by promoting the organization of excess food donations from various restaurants or homes. To this end, this system will ensure the fair allocation of this food to both individuals in need or charitable causes with respect for their dignity as recipients of the food.Objectives:To reduce the problem of food waste both in restaurants and at home.A new mobile app that will facilitate easier donations of foodstuffs that would otherwise go to waste, rather than discarding them.To facilitate a clear and organized procedure so that the needy charities can be given the needed food without any confusion or duplication.To establish an administrative system that can assist in dealing with users, monitoring operations, and in case of any conflicts regarding the distribution of food items.In order to be able to receive the food either for free or at a symbolic price in order to sustain the dignity of individuals in need.To create a system that is flexible and easy to use, which can be changed in the future and is environmentally sustainable.1.2 Overview of the technical areaThis project belongs to the technological area of distributed client–server architecture and mobile information systems. While the use of centralized server resources for data management and processing has been common for many years, modern applications are increasingly designed to operate on intelligent devices such as smartphones and web-based platforms. These systems are particularly effective in applications that require continuous data exchange and real-time interaction between multiple entities, which is a core requirement of the ZAD system [1]. This is attributed to various effects, especially on the environment, economy, and human beings. Massive amounts of food are lost each day by restaurants and homes all over the world. This does not only result in food wastage but also the wastage of resources or materials used to produce it, for example, water, energy, or land. Food waste or loss has been defined by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations as food that is intentionally discarded or fails to be used despite it being safe for human consumption [3]. In restaurants as well as in houses, food wastage normally takes place due to poor planning, which in turn leads to poor food management. In restaurants, wastage normally takes place due to meals being prepared in bulk, but there is no sale of food. At another level, food is normally being wasted at home because of people buying more than they can consume, resulting in poor storage of food. These cases clearly portray a challenge which can be taken care of by food redistribution.In addition, serious environmental problems arise from food waste. During landfill disposal, wasted food decomposes and generates methane gas, an extremely active greenhouse gas and one of the major contributors to climate change. Food loss and waste have been identified as responsible for a fair percentage share in global greenhouse gas emissions according to research [4], [5]. Therefore, the reduction of wasted food is another vital step toward environmental protection and supporting sustainability.2.2 Review of past/reported workSome authors have also carried out research on food loss/waste involving food restaurants, citing that food loss/waste happens at different food moments, including food preparation, food serving, and food left by customers. Research indicates food loss/waste can be reduced through better planning by managing food excess.In the modern world, various digital technologies and applications, especially in relation to food, have become a vital tool in combating food waste. Applications like Too Good To Go help restaurants sell their food at a discounted price, as opposed to simply throwing them away. Studies indicate that such applications motivate consumers to buy food in excess and thus become more environmentally conscious in their consumption habits, which may help in reducing food waste in a specific marketplace [6].Other studies have focused on determining the factors influencing the acceptance of food waste reduction platforms. The findings have indicated that simplicity, affordability, and ease of use are important factors influencing the acceptance or adoption of reduction platforms by users. However, the current platforms have mainly focused on restaurants while ignoring other kinds of organizations and administrative controls in ensuring equitable distribution and preventing conflict situations.Based on the literature review, the project aims to expand the systems by including households, charities, individuals, and an administrative role for better management of operations and conflict resolution. This will provide a more holistic and socially responsible system for the reduction of food waste.2.2.1 Comparison TableTable 1: Comparison of Surplus Food Reduction ApplicationsFeatureZAD (Our App)Forsa MENAToo Good To GoOLIOFlashfoodLhaq HalakTarget RegionWest bank(Ramallah initially)MENA (Jordan focus)Europe, USAGlobal (UK-based)North AmericaPalestine (Bethlehem)Requires Account CreationTrueTrueTrueTrueTrueTrueTargets Food WasteTrueTrueTrueTrueTrueTrueSupports RestaurantsTruePartialTruePartialTruePartialSupports Individuals as DonorsTrueFalseFalseTrueFalseTrueSupports Charitable OrganizationsTrueFalseFalseFalseFalseTrueFree Food DonationsTrueFalseFalseTrueFalseTrueLow-Price OffersTrueTrueTrueFalseTrueFalseQR Code PickupTrueFalseTrueFalseFalseFalseOnline PaymentTrueTrueTrueFalseTrueFalseCash on PickupTrueFalseFalseFalseFalseTrueLocation-Based SearchTrueTrueTrueTrueTrueFalseRating & FeedbackTrueFalseTrueTrueFalseFalseData Tracking & ReportsTrueTruePartialFalsePartialFalseMobile ApplicationTrueTrueTrueTrueTrueTrueWeb DashboardTrueTrueTrueFalseTrueFalseCommunity-BasedTrueFalseFalseTrueFalseTrue2.3 Tools and TechnologyA range of new tools and technologies will be employed in designing and developing the proposed application for reducing food waste, termed as “ZAD”. The system connects restaurants, individuals, and charitable organizations with people in need, in order to minimize food loss and promote efficient utilization of available resources.Restaurants, individuals, and charitable organizations can publish surplus food items through the system instead of discarding them. These items may be offered either free of charge or at a symbolic price while preserving the dignity of beneficiaries. Individuals may participate as donors or beneficiaries, and charitable organizations can act as both receivers and providers of surplus food. Users can browse available food packages based on their geographical location, reserve items, and collect them within a specified time window.The system supports four main actors: users, restaurants, charitable organizations, and administrators, each with a dedicated interface. Administrators are responsible for monitoring system activities, managing operations, and resolving potential conflicts. The system follows a client–server architecture, where the mobile application acts as the client and the backend server handles data management and system logic. In addition, a web-based dashboard is provided for restaurants, charitable organizations, and administrators to facilitate convenient management of listings, requests, and operational tasks using desktop or laptop devices. Both the mobile application and the web dashboard communicate with the same backend services through secure APIs. A conflict management mechanism is implemented to ensure fair and organized food distribution.3.1.1 System ObjectivesThe main objectives of the proposed system are as follows:To reduce food waste by enabling the sharing and redistribution of surplus food among different stakeholders.To provide a unified digital platform that connects restaurants, individuals, and charitable organizations with people in need.To facilitate organized donation, reservation, and collection of surplus food through mobile and web-based interfaces.To support both free and symbolic-price food distribution while preserving the dignity of beneficiaries.To allow charitable organizations to act as both receivers and providers of surplus food.To ensure fair and efficient allocation of food resources through conflict management mechanisms.To provide administrators with tools to monitor system activities and manage operations effectively.To enhance accessibility to surplus food by utilizing location-based services.3.1.2 System Main Features3.1.3 Operating EnvironmentsThe system operates within both a mobile environment and a web-based environment in order to support different types of users. End users interact with the system through a mobile application, while restaurants, charitable organizations, and administrators access the system through a web-based dashboard. This multi-platform design ensures ease of access and continuous system availability with high reliability.The mobile environment allows users to access the system using modern smartphones. The mobile application utilizes location-based services (GPS) to display nearby food providers and available surplus food packages, enabling users to locate and reserve food efficiently.The web-based platform allows administrators, restaurants, and charitable organizations to log in using standard web browsers. This interface provides a convenient way to manage food listings, reservations, users, and system operations through desktop or laptop devices.In addition, the system requires a stable internet connection to operate properly, as all system activities depend on data exchange between the clients and the backend server. The backend server is responsible for handling data storage, processing requests, and managing system logic.3.1.4 ConstrainsThe proposed system is subject to several constraints that may affect its operation and overall performance. These constraints are mainly related to technical limitations, user behavior, and operational conditions.Internet Connectivity: The system depends on a stable internet connection for both the mobile application and the web-based dashboard. Limited or unstable connectivity may affect system availability and delay data synchronization between clients and the backend server.GPS Accuracy: Location-based services rely on GPS functionality, which may sometimes provide inaccurate location data due to environmental conditions or device limitations. This may affect the accuracy of nearby food listings.Limited Pickup Time Window:Surplus food items must be collected within predefined time windows. Failure to collect food on time may result in automatic cancellation of reservations and potential food loss.User Commitment and Reliability:The effectiveness of the system depends on the commitment of users, donors, and organizations to follow reservation rules and pickup schedules. No-show behavior may reduce system efficiency.System Scalability:As the number of restaurants, individuals, and charitable organizations increases, the system may face challenges related to performance, response time, and resource management.Data Security and Privacy:The system must ensure secure handling of sensitive data related to users and organizations. Security requirements may impose constraints on data accessibility and system functionality.Conflict Management Complexity:Managing conflicts such as multiple requests for the same food package may introduce additional processing complexity and require careful system control mechanisms.3.1.5 User RequirementsUser requirements define the main functionalities that the system must provide in order to support users, restaurants, charitable organizations, and administrators in performing their tasks.1. Each layer has a specific responsibility and communicates with the adjacent layers through well-defined interfaces. This design improves modularity, scalability, maintainability, and system clarity.UI Layer (Presentation Layer)The UI Layer is responsible for all user interactions with the system. It provides mobile and web-based interfaces for individuals, restaurants, charitable organizations, and administrators. This layer allows users to perform actions such as authentication, browsing and searching food offers, viewing offer details, making reservations, selecting payment methods, tracking reservation status, confirming pickup using QR codes, submitting donations, and providing ratings or complaints.The main UI components include:Authentication InterfaceOffer Browsing & Offer Details InterfacesReservation & Reservation Status InterfacesQR InterfaceMap & Navigation InterfaceRating & Feedback InterfaceReport / Complaint InterfaceProvider Portal Interface (for restaurants and charities)Admin Dashboard InterfacePayment Method, Checkout, and Payment Status InterfacesThe UI layer does not contain business logic; it only collects input from users and displays System responses.Business Logic Layer (Application Layer)The Business Logic Layer contains the core processing logic of the system and enforces all business rules. It handles the coordination between different actors and manages system workflows.The main components of this layer include:Auth & RBAC Component: manages authentication and role-based access control.Provider & User Management Component: manages users, restaurants, and charitable organizations.Offer Management Component: manages food offers and surplus packages.Reservation Management Component: handles reservations and conflict prevention.QR Management Component: generates and validates QR codes for pickup confirmation.Pricing Rules Component: enforces low-price and free-offer policies.Payment Management Component: handles online and cash payments.Notification Component: sends system notifications.Map/Location Integration Component: integrates location-based services.Trust & Support Component: manages ratings, complaints, and reports.Admin Services Component: provides administrative control and monitoring features.This layer is the brain of the system and ensures correct execution of all system processes.Infrastructure Layer (Data & External Services Layer)The Infrastructure Layer is responsible for data storage and integration with external systems. It does not contain business logic and has no direct user interface.This layer includes:MySQL Database: stores all system data such as users, restaurants, charities, offers, reservations, payments, donations, ratings, and complaints.Firebase Services: provides authentication support and real-time notifications.Maps / Location API: provides geolocation and navigation services.External Payment Gateway: processes online payments and returns transaction status.This layer ensures data consistency, reliability, and secure integration with external services.Benefits of the Layered ArchitectureThe layered architecture provides several key benefits:Modularity: Each layer can be modified independently with minimal impact on other layers.Scalability: Layers can be scaled separately based on system load.Maintainability: Clear separation of concerns simplifies debugging and future updates.Flexibility: Infrastructure technologies (e.g., databases or APIs) can be replaced without affecting application logic.Reusability: Components can be reused and extended in future system enhancements./Figure 12: ZAD Architecture Diagram3.4.3 Deployment diagramThe deployment diagram shows how the ZAD system is physically deployed in a distributed client–server environment.
